# CRWN Pricing, Packaging, Onboarding, and Upgrade Strategy

**Prepared for:** CRWN
**Purpose:** Define the recommended artist platform pricing, feature packaging, onboarding path, upgrade logic, and economic measurement framework for CRWN's current ideal customer profile.

> Committed to the repo 2026-07-31 as the canonical pricing source (implemented the same day: see docs/crwn-brain/07-BUSINESS-RULES.md section 2 and CHANGELOG).

---

## 1. Executive Summary

CRWN's current pricing is misaligned with its ideal customer profile.

The current ICP is not a beginner artist looking for a cheap creator tool. It is an established artist who:

- Has roughly 250,000 to 2 million followers
- Has roughly 100,000 to 2 million monthly Spotify listeners
- Has 50 or more released songs
- Has already proven fans will spend money directly
- Uses a fragmented combination of tools such as Patreon, Shopify, Discord, Linktree, Kajabi, Gumroad, Eventbrite, Calendly, Stripe, email tools, and YouTube Memberships
- Needs consolidation, monetization, fan ownership, automation, migration, and operational leverage

CRWN should therefore be priced and packaged as a direct-to-fan operating system, not as a low-cost music upload utility.

### Recommended pricing structure

| Plan | Monthly Price | Platform Fee | Core Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Launch | $0 | 12% | Prove the first direct-to-fan offer |
| Pro | $49 | 8% | Operate a serious direct-to-fan business |
| Scale | $199 | 5% | Run a higher-volume artist business with a team |
| Label | Custom, later | Negotiated | Manage multiple artists and organizational complexity |

### Recommended onboarding philosophy

- Every artist account technically begins on Launch.
- CRWN recommends Launch, Pro, or Scale based on the artist's expected revenue, catalog, audience, team, migration needs, and desired features.
- The artist can build and preview the full system before paying.
- The primary upgrade decision happens at the launch boundary.
- Future upgrade prompts should be triggered by economics, feature intent, or actual limits.
- CRWN should never rely on random or repetitive upgrade popups.

The central product message should be:

> Build free. Choose your operating plan when you launch. Upgrade later when the math or your workflow says you should.

# 2. Current ICP

## Core ICP definition

CRWN's first ideal customer is:

> An artist who has already proven they can get fans to spend money directly, but whose monetization stack is fragmented.

### Target profile

- 250,000 to 2 million total followers
- 100,000 to 2 million monthly Spotify listeners
- 50 or more released songs
- At least one proven direct revenue stream outside streaming
- Three or more years of releasing music
- Meaningful fan engagement
- Existing superfans, repeat buyers, live viewers, email contacts, Discord members, merch buyers, ticket buyers, or VIP customers

### Strong buying signals

- Sells merch
- Runs meet-and-greets
- Has a fan email list
- Has a Discord community
- Uses YouTube Community
- Goes live consistently
- Has a manager or operating team
- Has sold VIP experiences
- Has used Patreon or memberships
- Has sold digital products
- Has a deep catalog
- Has repeat buyers

### Why this matters for pricing

This artist is not choosing between $0 and $10 based on affordability.

They are choosing based on:

- Revenue upside
- Migration effort
- Team workflow
- Reliability
- Fan ownership
- Lower transaction costs
- Consolidation of existing software
- Time saved
- Ability to fulfill recurring fan promises

# 3. First-Principles Pricing Logic

## Truth 1: The ICP already pays for software

A qualified artist may already pay for several of the following:

- Patreon
- Shopify
- Discord tools
- Email marketing
- Link-in-bio tools
- Calendly
- Eventbrite
- Community software
- Landing page tools
- Gumroad
- Kajabi
- Membership software
- Stripe-related tools

CRWN replaces or consolidates parts of that stack while adding new monetization systems.

Therefore, CRWN should be priced relative to:

- Software replaced
- Revenue created
- Fees saved
- Labor reduced
- Data consolidated
- Operational risk reduced

## Truth 2: Transaction fee reductions have substantial value

At $10,000 in monthly GMV:

| Platform Fee | CRWN Fee Revenue |
|---|---:|
| 12% | $1,200 |
| 8% | $800 |
| 5% | $500 |

At $50,000 in monthly GMV:

| Platform Fee | CRWN Fee Revenue |
|---|---:|
| 12% | $6,000 |
| 8% | $4,000 |
| 5% | $2,500 |

A fee reduction is highly valuable to the artist and costly for CRWN to provide. It should not be unlocked for $9.99 per month.

## Truth 3: The free plan should prove value

The free plan should allow the artist to experience the first useful outcome:

> I built a direct-to-fan offer, launched it, and received a paid transaction.

The free plan should not be generous enough to permanently operate a large artist business.

## Truth 4: Paid plans should unlock scale and leverage

Paid plans should be differentiated through:

- Lower transaction fees
- Automation
- Unlimited scale
- Live programming
- Advanced marketing
- Team workflows
- Migration help
- Support
- Reporting
- Operational control

# 4. Recommended Platform Plans

## 4.1 Launch

**Price:** $0 per month
**Platform fee:** 12%

### Purpose

Allow an artist to build, launch, and prove the first direct-to-fan offer.

### Recommended Launch features

- Up to four total fan-facing membership tiers
  - One free tier
  - Up to three paid tiers
- Up to 50 tracks
- Up to 250 members or contacts
- Artist page
- Track sales
- Album sales
- Fan subscriptions
- Basic digital and physical products
- At least one basic experience type
- Stripe Connect
- Basic fan CRM
- Basic earnings view
- Basic analytics
- CSV fan import with consent attestation
- One email campaign per month
- Referral links
- Standard payouts
- Basic Rise Mode
- Basic onboarding
- Basic community support
- CRWN branding

### Features not included on Launch

- Unlimited tracks
- Unlimited members
- Advanced bundles
- Recurring live experiences
- Executive Producer sessions
- Advanced scheduling
- Automated sequences
- Advanced segmentation
- Direct messaging
- Clipper campaigns
- Share-to-Earn
- Discount codes
- Advanced team splits
- Granular team permissions
- Advanced reporting
- Assisted migration
- Priority support

### Why 50 tracks instead of 20

The ICP commonly has at least 50 tracks. A 20-track limit prevents the target customer from evaluating CRWN with a representative catalog.

The free plan should allow a meaningful test without supporting unlimited scale.

## 4.2 Pro

**Recommended price:** $49 per month
**Platform fee:** 8%

### Purpose

Operate a serious direct-to-fan artist business.

### Recommended Pro features

Everything in Launch, plus:

- Up to four total fan-facing membership tiers
- Unlimited tracks
- Unlimited members
- Full subscriptions
- Paid vaults
- Products
- Bundles
- Live experiences
- Executive Producer sessions
- Content scheduling
- Offer scheduling
- Promise Calendar
- Direct messaging
- Fan referrals
- Share-to-Earn
- Clipper campaigns
- Discount codes
- Ten to twenty email campaigns per month
- Automated email sequences
- Fan segmentation
- Advanced analytics
- Revenue attribution
- Abandoned-cart recovery
- Limited team members
- Basic team splits
- Calendar integration
- Data export
- Standard human support
- Optional removal of CRWN branding when supported

### Economic break-even versus Launch

Launch charges 12%. Pro charges 8% plus $49.

Break-even:

```text
$49 / 4% = $1,225 monthly GMV
```

Above approximately $1,225 per month in CRWN sales, Pro costs the artist less than Launch.

### Annual option

Recommended:

- $490 per year

This provides approximately two months free and improves CRWN's cash collection and retention.

**Recommended price:** $199 per month
**Platform fee:** 5%

### Purpose

Support a higher-volume artist business with greater operational complexity.

### Recommended Scale features

Everything in Pro, plus:

- Higher or unlimited email limits with anti-abuse controls
- Advanced automations
- Advanced segmentation
- Advanced cohort and revenue analytics
- Custom reporting
- Larger team
- Granular permissions
- Approval workflows
- Expanded team splits
- Priority support
- Assisted fan migration

- Patreon tier mapping
- Shopify or Gumroad migration when built
- Advanced exports
- Account health monitoring
- Quarterly strategy review
- Faster human-support response
- More complex fulfillment and Promise Calendar workflows

### Economic break-even versus Pro

Pro costs $49 plus 8%. Scale costs $199 plus 5%.

Break-even:

```text
($199 - $49) / 3% = $5,000 monthly GMV
```

Above approximately $5,000 per month in CRWN sales, Scale costs the artist less than Pro.

### Annual option

Recommended:

- $1,990 per year

# 6. Onboarding Strategy

## Core rule

Every artist account technically begins on Launch.

CRWN separately stores the recommended operating plan.

Suggested data model:

```text
current_plan = launch
recommended_plan = pro
recommendation_reason = expected_revenue_and_features
projected_monthly_gmv = 3400
```

This allows the artist to build and preview the system without being charged.

## Why not start directly on Pro or Scale

Starting everyone directly on a paid plan would:

- Add friction before value is clear
- Reduce signup completion
- Force a pricing choice before the artist understands the product
- Prevent CRWN from using the completed setup as the sales mechanism
- Increase refund and cancellation risk

The correct approach is:

> Start technically on Launch, but personalize onboarding around the recommended plan.

# 10. Build Before Paying

Artists should be allowed to configure paid-plan features while technically on Launch.

Each feature should be marked clearly:

- Included with Launch
- Requires Pro at launch
- Requires Scale at launch

Example:

> Add monthly live listening session
> Pro feature. Configure it now and activate it when you launch.

CRWN should preserve all paid-feature configuration as drafts.

Do not:

- Delete the artist's work
- Force the artist to rebuild after upgrading
- Block them before they understand the feature
- Hide the plan requirement until the final second

# 11. Upgrade Moments

CRWN should use three upgrade moments.

## 11.1 Recommendation moment

### Timing

After the calculator and offer builder, before signup or immediately after account restoration.

### Purpose

- Explain the recommended plan
- Show the financial logic
- Set expectations
- Personalize onboarding

### Behavior

- Advisory
- Not a blocking modal
- Primary button continues onboarding
- Secondary action opens plan details

---

## 11.2 Launch-boundary upgrade

This is the primary paid conversion moment.

### Trigger

Artist clicks:

> Launch my CRWN

### The artist should already have

- Built fan tiers
- Imported catalog
- Imported fans
- Previewed the page
- Seen the Promise Calendar
- Built the launch campaign
- Connected or prepared Stripe
- Selected the desired features

### Example launch-boundary message

> Your CRWN is ready to launch.
>
> Your setup uses Pro features:
>
> - 82 uploaded tracks
> - Monthly live listening sessions
> - Scheduled Vault releases
> - Automated welcome sequence
> - Share-to-Earn campaign
>
> Based on your projected $3,400 in monthly revenue, Pro is estimated to save you $87 per month compared with Launch.

### Actions

- Launch with Pro
- Modify setup
- Launch with limited features

Do not silently remove paid features. Preserve them as drafts if the artist chooses Launch.

---

## 11.3 Post-launch contextual upgrades

After launch, upgrade prompts should only appear when:

- The artist attempts to use a paid feature
- The artist approaches a real plan limit
- The next plan becomes economically cheaper
- Their team or operational complexity grows

No random upgrade modals.

# 12. Pro Upgrade Triggers

## Revenue-based triggers

### At $750 trailing 30-day GMV

Quiet dashboard card:

> You are approaching the point where Pro may cost less than Launch.

### At $1,000 trailing 30-day GMV

Stronger dashboard recommendation:

> At approximately $1,225 in monthly sales, Pro becomes less expensive than Launch.

Show a one-time modal:

> Pro now costs you less.

> Based on your last 30 days of sales, upgrading would reduce your estimated monthly CRWN cost.

- Keep a persistent dashboard card

- Create recurring live programming
- Create an Executive Producer session
- Launch a clipper campaign
- Launch Share-to-Earn
- Create a bundle
- Create an automated email sequence
- Create a discount code
- Access advanced analytics

### Example

> Scheduling is included with Pro.
>
> You have built three upcoming Vault drops. Upgrade to schedule them automatically, or save them as drafts and publish manually.

# 14. Limit Warning System

Do not wait until the artist unexpectedly hits a limit.

Use:

- 70% usage: quiet indicator
- 85% usage: visible dashboard warning
- 100% usage: block new additions and preserve existing content

### Example for 50-track Launch limit

- 35 tracks: 15 tracks remaining
- 43 tracks: Pro recommendation appears
- 50 tracks: new uploads blocked, upgrade offered

### Bulk-import behavior

Before upload begins, inspect the number of selected tracks.

Example:

> Your catalog contains 82 tracks. You can import 50 on Launch or import the full catalog with Pro.

Do not reveal the limit after the upload has already started.

# 17. Core Business Metrics

## 17.1 Activation

Do not define activation as account creation.

Recommended activation definition:

> Artist publishes a CRWN page, connects Stripe, imports fans, and receives the first paid fan transaction.

### Activation funnel

1. Calculator completed
2. Account created
3. Email verified
4. Offer restored
5. Catalog imported
6. Fans imported
7. Stripe connected
8. Page launched
9. First invitation sent
10. First fan joined
11. First paid transaction

## 17.2 Drop-off

Measure conversion between every step.

Highest-risk steps:

- Signup
- Email verification
- Catalog migration
- Fan import
- Stripe Connect
- Offer configuration
- Publishing
- Sending the first invitation

## 17.3 CAC to LTGP

Use lifetime gross profit, not only lifetime revenue.

```text
Lifetime gross profit =
(monthly subscription margin + monthly fee margin)
x expected customer lifetime
```

Then compare:

```text
CAC : LTGP
```

A reasonable early target is at least approximately 1:3.

## 17.4 CAC payback

```text
CAC payback months =
CAC / monthly gross profit per artist
```

Include:

- Founder sales time
- Research
- Outreach software
- Calls
- Travel
- Onboarding
- Sales commissions
- Migration labor
- Follow-up time

## 17.5 COGS

Track by plan:

- Hosting
- Storage
- Audio delivery
- Video and live-streaming cost
- Email cost
- AI usage
- Support
- Migration labor
- Refunds
- Disputes
- Payout costs
- Affiliate payments
- Sales commissions

Scale pricing must cover its higher support and migration burden.

## 17.6 Churn

Track:

### Logo churn

Percentage of artists who cancel.

### Revenue churn

Percentage of recurring CRWN revenue lost.

### GMV churn

Amount of artist sales volume that stops processing through CRWN.

An artist can remain subscribed while no longer using CRWN meaningfully. GMV churn identifies this.

## 17.7 Net revenue retention

```text
NRR =
(starting revenue + expansion - contraction - churn)
/ starting revenue
```

Include:

- Subscription revenue
- Transaction-fee revenue
- Plan upgrades
- GMV expansion
- GMV contraction

## 17.8 Referral rate

Track separately:

- Artist-to-artist referrals
- Fan-to-fan referrals
- Clipper-driven acquisition
- Manager and partner referrals

Artist referral rate:

```text
activated referred artists
/ activated artists eligible to refer
```

A referral should count only when the referred artist reaches a meaningful activation event.

## 17.9 Contribution margin

Track contribution margin by plan and GMV cohort.

Suggested cohorts:

- Under $500 monthly GMV
- $500 to $2,500
- $2,500 to $10,000
- $10,000 to $50,000
- Above $50,000

Do not rely on one blended LTV or margin number.

- GMV after upgrade

## Key pricing questions to answer

- Does $49 improve revenue without reducing activation?
- Does Scale attract real high-volume artists?
- Are artists upgrading because of savings or features?
- Which features cause the highest upgrade rate?
- Does assisted migration increase activation enough to justify its cost?
- What percentage of artists reach $1,225 GMV?
- What percentage reach $5,000 GMV?
- How long does each threshold take?
- Does upgrading improve retention?
- Does annual billing reduce churn?
- Does a lower platform fee increase artist GMV enough to offset the reduction?
