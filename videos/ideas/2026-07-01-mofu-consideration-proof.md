# MOFU Consideration Ideas: "It Actually Works For Someone Like Me"

Generated 2026-07-01. Corrected 2026-07-01 after a full read of the 90-day calendar (see dedup notes). **Separate middle-of-funnel track, NOT a merge into the 90-day awareness calendar.**

Where TOFU (the 385-script awareness library) makes an artist *problem-aware* ("the industry is broken, you should own your fans") and BOFU (the 2026-06-25 batch) tells an already-signed artist *what to do Monday morning*, this track lives in between. The viewer already knows CRWN and the direct-to-fan idea exist. They are not sold. Every idea here names the **specific doubt** keeping a warm-but-unconvinced indie artist from acting, then **reverses it with proof plus mechanism**, anchored to a real, current hip-hop/R&B artist at or near the avatar's own scale. Capability each demos shown after the arrow (→).

**Placement:** run as its own consideration series, not the 3/day rotation. Best uses: retarget anyone who watched a TOFU "why" video but hasn't signed up, page highlights, and the "still on the fence?" DM. Per `feedback_carousel_vs_reel_audience`, most of these are proof/how-it-works beats aimed at people who already follow you, so the ones marked **[carousel-fit]** convert better as a swipe carousel to warm followers than as a cold reel.

**Structure rule (this batch's whole point):** injustice FIRST, then the proof. Reach = gate(skip) × fuel(share+save), and injustice is the gate. Pure positivity ("this artist is winning") has no gate and dies (`feedback_shortform_performance_model`, IDEA_SCORECARD). So every idea opens on the trap the system runs on an artist, then lands the proof that someone at your scale beat it. That's the #2 / #21 winning arc (wound → reversal), NOT a two-artist "same/different" contrast (`feedback_shortform_quality`) — it's one artist's trap-to-escape line.

## Doubt 1 — "I need a hit / I need to be famous first"

1. [S] The usual arc: you go viral, a label waves a check, you sign, and now they own the masters AND the fans the moment brought you. Hype cools, they drop you, and you can't even reach the people who found you. Anycia broke the arc. Same viral snippet in 2023, same Drake and Cole co-signs, but she stayed independent and kept her masters and her audience. The move on CRWN: when the moment hits, the fans land on a page YOU own. → owned page / capture **[carousel-fit]**

## Doubt 2 — "Only rich or already-famous artists can go independent"

2. [S] The advance feels like belief in you. It's the price they pay to own you: recouped out of your own sales, and they keep the masters after you've paid it back. BigXthaPlug went the other way: put it out through a distributor (UnitedMasters), kept his masters instead of signing them away. "Take Care" did 48,000 week one and passed 500,000 units, and he keeps more than signed artists his size. Independence wasn't his reward for making it. It's how he made it. → keep more of the dollar / ownership **[carousel-fit]**

3. [S] They sell you the story that you need a team, a label, a machine, so that going alone feels impossible, and that "help" is what they trade for ownership. MIKE called the bluff. Built his following himself in the NYC underground, then ran the whole thing as his own label (10k) with the [sLUms] crew, no major. The infrastructure they charge your masters for is one page now. → community / share-to-earn

## Doubt 3 — "Fans won't actually pay ME"

4. [S] Streaming trained you to believe your fans are worth pennies. 15,000 of them looks like nothing on a royalty statement, a fraction of a cent a play. Ryan Leslie texted those exact same 15,000 directly and sold 200 tickets at $1,700 each in 48 hours. Same fans. The only thing that changed was who owned the line to them. → SMS + one-time experiences

5. [S] "My fans are broke, they won't pay." That's the streaming math in your head, the one that prices a fan at $0.003 so you never even ask. Run the real number: the 50 who comment on everything, at $15 a month, is $9,000 a year, off people who were already there for free. The fan was always worth more than the stream priced them. → tiers **[carousel-fit]**

## Doubt 4 — "My following is too small to matter"

6. [S] The industry grades you on follower count and stream totals. Vanity numbers they own, can inflate, and can switch off the day you stop being useful. Anycia charted off a modest core she actually owned. The reversal: you're counting the wrong thing. A big rented audience that only streams loses to a small one that pays. Followers are a maybe. A paid fan is a yes. → tiers / owned line **[carousel-fit]**

7. [Y] "Can an artist with 10,000 followers actually replace a label check?" Start with the trap: the check comes with them keeping your masters and your fans. Then the math that beats it, through three current independents at that scale (BigXthaPlug's keep, Anycia's ownership, Ryan Leslie's 15,000 fans doing $2M). Not the streaming number. The direct number: tiers, one-time experiences, and what a fan is worth when you own the line instead of renting it. → full model

## Doubt 5 — "It's too complicated, I'm not a business person"

8. [S] The algorithm decides who sees you, and it can bury you the week you need it most. That's the platform's leverage, and it's dressed up as "you just have to crack the algorithm." Ryan Leslie never played that game. His entire $2M album cycle ran off one thing: a list of phone numbers he could text. No algorithm in the middle. On CRWN, capturing that list is the first button, not a course. → SMS capture

9. [S] "I need a whole distribution and marketing operation" is the story that makes ownership feel out of reach, so you hand it over for someone else to run. MIKE's operation is basically himself: he makes it, puts it out through his own label, and sells straight to the fans who found him online. You don't build a company. You open one page and put one link in your bio. → owned page / smart link **[carousel-fit]**

## Doubt 6 — "I'd lose the reach a label gives me"

10. [S] The label's whole pitch is "we get you everywhere." But a distributor already puts you on every DSP while you keep your masters, which is exactly how BigXthaPlug and Anycia are on every platform, independent. Playlist reach was never the label's secret. The direct line to your fans is the part they actually keep, forever, and the part you can own instead. → owned line / capture **[carousel-fit]**

## Doubt 7 — "Is the money actually real at my scale, or is this hype"

11. [S] Here's the part that should make you mad: the artist with the bigger deal usually takes home less. Signed acts twice their size keep a fraction. Ryan Leslie pulled $2M from 15,000 fans. BigXthaPlug moved 500,000 units independent and keeps more than majors his size. Anycia charted owning her masters. None of them are Drake. All of them out-kept the people who signed. → keep more of the dollar **[carousel-fit]**

## Closer

12. [S] Right now, by default, the fans you're making belong to whoever you sign with next. Every drop, every new follower, you're building someone else's asset for free. Anycia, BigX, MIKE, Ryan Leslie were no bigger than you when they stopped the bleed and kept theirs. The page costs nothing to open. What costs you is another year of handing yours away. → owned page **[carousel-fit]**
