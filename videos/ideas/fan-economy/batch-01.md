# Fan Economy idea batch 01

Pitched 2026-08-20. Ten new case studies, no artist reused as subject or foil from scripts 1-30.
Four of ten are women. Ten distinct mechanisms, so the batch is not one idea with ten faces.

`/crwn-fan-economy-carousel`. Numbers below are UNVERIFIED by design: the script does the research.

**1. Mach-Hommy — he set the price himself**
Hook: Most rappers let the market decide what a record is worth. Mach-Hommy told it. So how many buyers does he actually need?
Withheld: what one copy costs
Reveal shape: units times the artist-set price, against a year of streaming
Wow: the price was never discovered, it was declared, and people paid it
Mechanism: direct premium pricing · Family: G · Magnet: vault-revenue-planner + VAULT
ICP: prolific independent, sells direct, proven buyers, fragmented stack
NUMBER: LIKELY

**2. Open Mike Eagle — the income that survives the album cycle**
Hook: He puts out a record when he feels like it. His fans pay him every month regardless. What is a month worth?
Withheld: the membership price times the count
Reveal shape: recurring monthly revenue against a release-driven year
Wow: the money does not stop when the promotion stops
Mechanism: recurring membership and LTV · Family: I · Magnet: vault-revenue-planner + VAULT
ICP: independent, established catalog, already runs a paid membership
NUMBER: NEEDS RESEARCH (tier prices are public, patron counts often are not)

**3. Alex Isley vs SZA — the room versus the stream**
Hook: SZA fills arenas. Alex Isley fills rooms that hold a few hundred, over and over. Which room pays more per person?
Withheld: the per-person economics of the small room
Reveal shape: capacity times ticket price per head, against per-listener streaming
Wow: the smaller room pays more per person and the people in it are reachable
Mechanism: live experience and ticketing · Family: G · Magnet: live-experience-calculator + LIVE
ICP: independent R&B, devoted audience, sells out repeatedly
Polarity: Alex wins on per-person economics. SZA's scale is credited, never mocked.
NUMBER: LIKELY (capacities and ticket prices are public)

**4. billy woods — the record was paid for before it existed**
Hook: He runs the label, presses the vinyl, and the fans pay before it ships. How much of a record can an audience fund?
Withheld: the preorder take before release day
Reveal shape: preorder units times price, against the cost of making it
Wow: the audience financed it, so no advance was needed and nothing was owed
Mechanism: preorders and demand-backed projects · Family: I · Magnet: proof-of-demand + DEMAND
ICP: owns his label, sells direct, deep catalog, proven buyers
NUMBER: NEEDS RESEARCH (prices are public, units are not)

**5. Destin Conrad — he knows the listeners, not the buyers**
Hook: Millions of people play his songs every month. He cannot name one of them. What is a name worth?
Withheld: how many of those listeners have ever bought anything
Reveal shape: the gap between audience size and identifiable buyers
Wow: the unknowability IS the finding, which is why the number is the point
Mechanism: fan identification · Family: G · Magnet: own-your-fans-calculator + OWN
ICP: large streaming audience, few owned relationships, fragmented stack
NUMBER: RISKY. The buyer count may not be public at all. The angle survives without it, because
"nobody can tell you this number, including him" is the lesson. Do not invent a figure to rescue it.

**6. Terrace Martin vs Metro Boomin — paid access to the producer**
Hook: Metro's drums are on everything. Terrace sells you the chair next to him. Which one earns more per fan?
Withheld: the price of a seat in the session
Reveal shape: seats times price, against per-stream production income
Wow: access is a product, and it is one only the artist can sell
Mechanism: Executive Producer Sessions (SHIPPED) · Family: G · Magnet: executive-producer-session + SESSION
ICP: producer-artist, independent, direct relationship with fans
Polarity: Terrace wins on revenue per fan. Metro's reach is credited.
NUMBER: RISKY (session pricing may not be public; verify before committing)

**8. Kari Faux — she asked before she printed**
Hook: Most artists guess what merch will sell and eat the boxes that do not. What does asking first actually save?
Withheld: the cost of the guess
Reveal shape: unsold inventory cost against a demand-tested run
Wow: the savings are invisible because they are losses that never happened
Mechanism: demand discovery · Family: G · Magnet: proof-of-demand + DEMAND
ICP: independent, direct merch seller, engaged audience
NUMBER: RISKY (fallback: frame as the cost of guessing, which is generic and still true)

**9. Bktherula — the list she owns versus the algorithm she rents**
Hook: Her reach is enormous and none of it belongs to her. What happens the day the feed changes?
Withheld: what share of her audience she could reach without a platform
Reveal shape: reachable owned audience against total platform audience
Wow: reach you cannot contact is reach you are renting
Mechanism: owned relationships and first-party data · Family: G · Magnet: own-your-fans-calculator + OWN
ICP: young, large streaming audience, platform-dependent, no owned list
NUMBER: RISKY
Caution: run the repost test hard here. The framing must be "this is what the industry set up",
never "she did it wrong".

Mechanism: membership tiers · Family: G · Magnet: vault-revenue-planner + VAULT
ICP: independent, direct merch and touring, visible repeat buyers
