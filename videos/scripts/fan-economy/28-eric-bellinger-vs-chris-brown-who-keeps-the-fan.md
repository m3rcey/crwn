# Eric Bellinger vs Chris Brown: who keeps the fan the song made

**SCRIPT:**

Whose fans pay more for the most personal thing they sell, Chris Brown's or Eric Bellinger's?
Let's find out.

Eric Bellinger came up writing. Usher, Justin Bieber, Big Time Rush, and Chris Brown, including New Flame.
He won his first Grammy off his contributions to Chris Brown's F.A.M.E.
He also runs his own company, All Wins, drops constantly, and sits around 3 million monthly listeners under his own name.

Now Chris Brown's side of this is not a knock at all. He's one of the biggest R&B artists of the last twenty years and those records are his.
When a song like that lands, a whole audience attaches to the ARTIST. That's how it works, that's how it should work, and the star earned that.

But watch what happens to the writer in that same moment.
The song blows up. Millions of people learn every word.
And every single one of them attaches to somebody else's name.

That aint a complaint about credit either. Credit is nice. Credit dont pay monthly.
So both of these men sell something to the fans who want to get closest, and they aint selling the same thing.

I put the two price tags next to each other and I had to read them twice.

Quick sidenote.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
The people who follow a writer are the deepest listeners in music, and most artists sell them one thing at one price and stop.
On the CRWN app the ladder goes UP: a rung at ten dollars and a rung at a thousand, so the fan who would pay five grand isnt capped by the cheapest thing you ever made.
ANYWAY.

So whose fans pay more for that one personal thing, Chris Brown's or Eric Bellinger's?
Chris Brown's meet and greet on his 11:11 tour was $1,111, and it reportedly sold out for the whole tour.
Eric Bellinger's top membership tier lets a fan buy an original hook from him for $5,000.
So one Eric Bellinger hook costs about as much as four and a half Chris Brown meet and greets.
But Chris Brown sold his out city after city, and nobody has said how many hooks Eric Bellinger has sold yet.

The number is not the story though.
A meet and greet sells a fan a moment with the star. The hook sells a fan the same skill that helped make the star's records.
And every song Eric Bellinger ever wrote for somebody else is still out there advertising that skill, for free, and he did that labor a decade ago.

To be fair, them prices come from a 2024 tour and a launch announcement, not anybody's books.
And selling out a meet and greet in every city is the harder thing to build. Chris Brown built it.
So if you already sell to your own people and your top price is a thirty dollar shirt:
your catalog is already promoting you in rooms you will never stand in, and you have never once found out what the top of your own fanbase would pay. What would your best buyer spend to be part of the next record?

I built a free calculator that shows what the highest-value end of your fanbase would pay for a real part in the work.
