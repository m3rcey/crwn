# Benny vs Post Malone: they bought it twice

**SCRIPT:**

Whose fans pay more for one copy of a record, Post Malone's or Benny the Butcher's?
Let's find out.

Post Malone has more than sixty million monthly listeners. That is an audience the size of a country, and about as much reach as this business has to give.
And he sells records to them too, straight off his own official store.

Benny the Butcher runs a different model. His first Stabbed & Shot record with 38 Spesh went out of stock.
Then the sequel came, and it didn't come cheaper. It came in a stack of versions, and some of them were a lot rarer than others.

Now here's the thing nobody puts side by side.
One of these men can sell a record to sixty million people. The other one is selling to the people who wanted the first one.
You would think the price follows the size of the crowd. I did too.

Hold that thought.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
A listener can only listen. A buyer can buy again, and buy up.
The CRWN app is built for exactly that: tiers your fans join, and a vault behind one of them.
ANYWAY.

So whose fans really pay more for one copy, Post Malone's or Benny the Butcher's?
Post Malone's F-1 Trillion is $45.99 on his official store, and that is a double record. Nothing on the record shelf there costs more.
Benny's regular copies of Stabbed & Shot 2 run $29.98 to $34.98.
So on a regular copy Post Malone gets more, and he can sell it to sixty million people.
But the store selling Benny's record listed five test pressings at $250 each, and every one of them is sold out.
One of those cost more than five of Post Malone's double records put together.

Now watch what that actually means.
The first Stabbed & Shot sold out at $24.98. The sequel went up to $34.98 on a regular copy and $250 on the rarest one, and people kept buying.
Nobody raises the price on people who barely wanted it the first time.

Now to be fair, five test pressings is five people, and a collector's item aint a normal record. Post Malone's reach is worth an enormous amount in ways a vinyl run never is, and selling a $45.99 double record to an audience that size is the harder thing to build.
And a price ladder only works when the record is good enough that missing it hurts.

But if five people paid $250 for a record anybody can stream, the question aint how many people heard it.
It's whether anybody in your world has ever been given a second thing to buy, at a higher price.

I built a free Vault Revenue Planner that shows whether your unreleased songs are enough to run a Vault, and what to charge for it.

Comment VAULT and I'll DM you the link.
