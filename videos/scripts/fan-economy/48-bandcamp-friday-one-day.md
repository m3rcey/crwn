# Bandcamp Friday: one day

**SCRIPT:**

One day a month, Bandcamp waives its cut and artists keep everything.
No discount for the fan. Nothing changes except who keeps the money. So how much do fans move in a single day?
The answer should change what you think fans are willing to do.

Let's break it down.

It started in March 2020 as an emergency thing. Shows had stopped, nobody could tour, and the idea was simple.
On these days the platform takes nothing. The fan pays the same price they'd pay any other day.
So there is no deal here, no sale, no bonus track. The only difference is that the artist keeps more.

Now here's the thing nobody puts side by side.
Everything in modern marketing says people respond to discounts. Take money off, get more sales.
This does the opposite. It asks people to pay full price on purpose, on a specific day, so somebody else does better.
Go guess what one of them days does before I say it.

Hold that thought.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
Most artists have never once told their people that buying direct pays them more, and then wonder why everybody streams instead.
That's what the CRWN app is built around: membership tiers and a vault your top members pay to get into.
ANYWAY.

So what do fans move on a single Bandcamp Friday?
Three point eight million dollars in one day, in the best one of 2025.

And here's the crazy part. Since 2020 these days have moved a hundred and fifty four million dollars to artists and labels, nineteen million of it in 2025 alone.
People organised their spending around a date, at full price, because somebody told them it mattered.
Nobody made them. There was no discount. They just knew.

Now to be fair, that money is spread across an enormous number of artists, so this is not three point eight million landing in somebody's account. Most of them got a handful of sales that day.
And people found most of that music through streaming and social first. Discovery did its job.

But if fans will pay full price on a specific day just so more of it reaches you, the question aint how do I get people to buy.
