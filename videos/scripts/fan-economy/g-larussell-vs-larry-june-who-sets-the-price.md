# LaRussell vs Larry June: who sets the price

**SCRIPT:**

Larry June puts a price on it and LaRussell lets the fan pick, and what the exact same album is worth under them two rules is a gap independent artists should see before they price anything else.

Here's the setup.

Larry June runs the model everybody knows, and he runs it clean.
Independent, his own lane, Midnight Organic, merch, the whole healthy-living brand.
Everything got a number on it. You want the hoodie, it's the hoodie price. Predictable money, and it works.

LaRussell went the other way completely.
Backyard shows in Vallejo where the ticket is pay what you want.
And he put his album out direct to fans the same way. No fixed price. Started at a dollar.
Every single fan could have paid a dollar and kept it moving.

So this is a real experiment, not a theory.
One artist decides what the fan pays. The other lets the fan decide.
And most of us been taught only one of them is safe.

About a thousand fans bought LaRussell's album in the first 24 hours.
Now, a normal independent album price is like ten bucks, so you already know what a thousand fans is worth under Larry June's rules.
What them same thousand fans did when nobody set a ceiling is the number I couldnt get over.

Your problem aint marketing to fans. It's that you need a market FOR fans.
A market means the ones that want to go deeper have somewhere deeper to go.
That's the whole point of a tier ladder on the CRWN app: a free rung, then real rungs above it, instead of one price for everybody.
ANYWAY.

Them thousand fans paid $57,000. In one day.
Same thousand people. At a fixed ten dollars that's $10,000.
Letting them choose produced almost six times the same crowd.

And one fan in there paid $11,000 by himself.
That's the part that should rewire how you think about a price tag.
A fixed price aint just a price, it's a ceiling AND a floor. It quietly turns away the fan who only had five dollars, and it caps the one who would have paid a thousand.
And that aint a Larry June thing, that's how every one of us was taught to price, me included.
Larry June's number is dependable. LaRussell's number found somebody nobody knew existed.

Now to be fair, LaRussell's way is riskier and Larry June's product business is real, predictable money you can plan a year around.
But if you're an independent artist selling everything at one price to everybody:
which one of your fans are you turning away, and which one are you capping?
