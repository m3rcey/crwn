# Del vs J. Cole: nine albums

**SCRIPT:**

Who has more albums out with no major label behind them, Del the Funky Homosapien or J Cole?
Let's find out.

Del the Funky Homosapien signed to Elektra and put out two albums, in 1991 and 1993. Then he left.
From 1997 he's been releasing through Hieroglyphics Imperium, the label he and his people built, and from 2009 a lot of it has been straight self-released.

J. Cole is one of the biggest rappers alive. Around thirty seven million people a month, give or take, depending on the week you check.
He built his own label too, Dreamville. He just built it inside the major label system.

Now here's the thing nobody puts side by side.
Monthly listeners are rented. They are counted for you by somebody else, and they can go down next month without you doing anything wrong.
A record you put out on your own label is still under your name, on your label, thirty years later.
These two made opposite bets on that, and I promise you'll guess at least one of the numbers wrong.

Hold that thought.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
Most artists are building the number somebody else keeps score of, and the parts they DO own, the records and the buyers, are scattered across companies that each hold one piece.
That's the whole idea behind the CRWN app: the catalog, the members and the money reconciling in one account with your name on it.
ANYWAY.

So who really has more albums out with no major label behind them, Del or J. Cole?
Nine of Del's eleven studio albums came out on his own label, self-released, or on an independent.
J. Cole has none of his seven. Every one came through Columbia or Interscope.
And every one of those seven debuted at number one on the Billboard 200. The latest one did 280,000 units its first week.
So J. Cole went through the majors and came out with seven number one albums, and Del walked away and came out with nine albums no major label ever touched.

And that's not even the interesting half.
Two of Del's albums belong to the deal he signed as a teenager. The other nine came after he left, and thirty years later they are still out on his terms.
There is no version of that where somebody drops him, and no quarter where the number falls off a cliff because a playlist changed.

Now to be fair, J. Cole's reach is real and enormous and it pays in ways nine indie albums never will. Seven number one albums is the harder thing to build, and thirty seven million people is not an accident.
And Del has never had to carry the costs Cole carries either.

But if the thing you keep is the thing you put out yourself, the question aint how do I grow the number.
It's that you been releasing and selling for years and nobody is watching the back end. How much of what you already made is really yours, and who is collecting what it earns?
