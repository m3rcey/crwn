# DJ Muggs vs DJ Khaled: a year with no permission

**SCRIPT:**

Which DJ gave his fans more albums in 2021, DJ Khaled or DJ Muggs?
Let's find out.

DJ Khaled puts out a record and a whole building moves behind it. Radio, features, a rollout, a date everybody agreed on months ago.
His model is the biggest version of the normal one. Assemble the stars, spend the budget, pick the quarter, push it everywhere at once.
It works. That is what a major label machine is FOR, and he is very good at operating it.

DJ Muggs came out of Cypress Hill and built Soul Assassins into his own label, out of his own studio.
A rapper turns up, they make a record, it goes out.
No single. No radio. No quarter. Just finished, then out.

Now here's the comparison nobody bothers to make.
One of them needs a building to say yes. The other one needs an afternoon.
Count what that difference turns into over one calendar year.

Hold that thought.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
Being able to release whenever is worth nothing if the people who'd buy it are spread across a store, a mailing list and a group chat that never agree on who a fan is.
On the CRWN app the cadence and the buyers live together: you drop to rungs, and the rungs know who's in them.
ANYWAY.

So which one gave his fans more albums in 2021, Khaled or Muggs?
Khaled put out one, Khaled Khaled, and it hit number one on the Billboard 200 and went platinum.
Muggs put out seven. Two solo records, and five with a different rapper each time.
Which means Muggs put out seven albums in the year Khaled put out one.
Khaled's one went further than any of those seven. Muggs's seven gave his fans something new about every seven weeks.

And here's what got me.
Not one of those seven needed a building to say yes. Several of them came out on his own label, Soul Assassins.
He was not waiting on a slot. There was no slot.

Now to be fair, one Khaled record reaches more people in a week than Muggs's albums reach in a year, and that machine puts artists in front of audiences no independent can buy their way to. That reach is the harder thing to build.
And seven albums a year only works because Muggs spent thirty years earning an audience that shows up without being told.

But if the whole difference is whether somebody has to approve you, the question aint how do I get in the building.
It's whether you could reach the people who already buy from you tomorrow, on purpose, without paying anybody for the privilege.

Comment VAULT and I'll DM you the link.
