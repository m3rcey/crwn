# redveil decides who hears it first, and that is worth money

**SCRIPT:**

redveil produces and releases everything himself, so he decides exactly who hears a song first, and what that one decision could be worth in a year is a number almost no artist ever prices.

Let's break it down.

redveil is from Prince George's County and he was making this stuff as a teenager.
Four albums, self released. Bittersweet Cry, Niagara, Learn 2 Swim, Sankofa. All of them self produced.
Toured with Denzel Curry, opened for Freddie Gibbs, showed up on records with Chance and JPEGMAFIA.
No label deciding a rollout. No committee. It's him.

So he already has the thing everybody says they want: total control of the release.
And like almost everybody who has it, he uses it to do the same thing a label would do.
Pick a Friday. Push the button. Everybody on earth gets it at the exact same second.

Now think about what that throws away.
Your day one fan, the one who has been there since a project nobody streamed, gets the song at the same moment as somebody who found you off a playlist that morning.
You had the one thing you can actually give a real fan that costs you nothing, and you gave it to everybody at once.

So run it the other way.
Your top rung hears it Monday. The next rung Wednesday. Everybody else Friday.
Same song, same week, no extra work. It just stops being one moment and starts being a ladder people have a reason to climb.

So what is being first actually worth to the people who care most? I put a number on it.

Quick sidenote.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
This one is real and running today: on the CRWN app a release can open per tier, days early, staggered down the ladder automatically.
You set who hears it when, one time, and the rollout runs itself.
ANYWAY.

So what is that first-listen decision worth in a year?

He's got about 107,000 people following him on X alone.
Say only 1 in 100 of them ever becomes a paying member. That's roughly 1,070 people.
And say 300 of them move up a rung, paying $15 more a month, just to be first.
That's $4,500 a month. $54,000 a year.

But the upgrade money aint the real prize.
Being first is the only reward in music that costs the artist nothing to give.
A vinyl costs money to press. A show costs money to put on. A shirt has to get made.
Hearing it Monday instead of Friday costs him zero, and to the person who has been there since the beginning it's worth more than the merch.
