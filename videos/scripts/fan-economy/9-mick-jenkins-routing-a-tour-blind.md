Mick Jenkins has run 40 plus cities on somebody's best guess about where his people are, and what his fans could put down BEFORE a single venue gets booked is the number that makes routing a tour a completely different job.

Mick Jenkins is Chicago, and he built one of the most locked-in fanbases in rap.
The Water[s], The Healing Component, Pieces of a Man, The Circus, and he has said plainly he aint interested in a major.
His people dont casually listen. He spent years training them to sit with metaphors and dig, and they do it.

And he did 40 plus cities on the Smoker's Club run.
Now ask the question nobody asks about a tour: how does anybody decide WHICH 40?
Some streaming heat map. Which markets a promoter can get. What worked last time. A feeling.

That's the whole industry, and it's a bet made with real money.
You book the room, you pay the deposit, you buy the routing, and THEN you find out if that city wanted you.
Some nights are religious experiences. Some nights are 60 people in a 900 cap room, and the artist eats that.

So flip the order.
What if the city had to raise its hand first?
Fans put a real deposit down for a show in they city. No pledge, no show. Enough hands, it gets booked, and everybody who put money down already has a ticket.
The tour stops being a bet and turns into a pre-order.

So how much money could be sitting there before anybody signs a venue contract?

Your problem aint marketing to fans. It's that you need a market FOR fans.
Now I want to be straight with you: routing a tour off fan deposits is NOT something I am telling you the CRWN app does today. It doesnt.
What does exist is the front half, testing whether the demand is real before you spend, instead of finding out after.
The full version is the kind of fan economy I think artists should be building, and it's the bigger idea behind what we building.
ANYWAY.

So what could be committed before a single venue is booked?

Say just 200 people in a city put $30 down to bring the show.
Across only 10 cities, that's 2,000 people and $60,000 committed, before a deposit, a bus, or a contract.

But the money is the small half of it.
He would also know, with receipts, which 10 cities to go to.
Right now the risk sits entirely on the artist, and the information sits with the fans. Nobody ever asked them, so it just stays there.
Flip that and the guess disappears, along with the 60-people night.

That's a model of a thing that mostly doesnt exist yet, so treat it as the idea, not a plan.
But if you're an independent artist about to book anything:
which of your cities could actually fill a room, and would you bet a deposit on your answer?
