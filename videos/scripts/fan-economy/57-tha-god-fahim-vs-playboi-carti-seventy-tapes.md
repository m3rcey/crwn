# Tha God Fahim vs Playboi Carti: seventy tapes

**SCRIPT:**

Whose fans had more new projects to buy between 2015 and 2018, Playboi Carti's or Tha God Fahim's?
Let's find out.

Playboi Carti's whole model is scarcity. Long waits, no explanation, the wait doing the marketing for him.
And it works, because when he does show up the entire internet stops.

Tha God Fahim went the opposite direction completely. Atlanta, no label deal, tape after tape, sold straight off his own Bandcamp page.
No rollout. No single. Just finish it and put it up for sale.
By the summer of 2018 he was moving so fast that Bandcamp's own editorial site wrote a guide to help people keep up.

Now here's the thing nobody puts side by side.
One of them makes you wait for every record. The other one never makes you wait at all.
Count what each one gave a fan to buy in the same three years.

Hold that thought.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
Seventy releases is seventy chances to learn who your repeat buyers are, and a page of sales tells you almost none of it.
On the CRWN app the buyer is the record: you can see who came back for the fourth one, and sell them a fifth on purpose.
ANYWAY.

So whose fans had more to buy in those three years, Carti's or Tha God Fahim's?
Carti put out two projects, his first mixtape and Die Lit, and Die Lit went top three on the Billboard 200.
Tha God Fahim was approaching seventy tapes, and every one of them sold off his own page.
Which means for every project Carti put out, Fahim put out about 35.
Carti's two went out to a massive audience all at once. Fahim's seventy had to find their buyers one sale at a time.

The number is not the story though.
Every one of those tapes was a sale, not a stream. Seventy releases worth of people who paid him directly, on a page where the buyers show up as people and not play counts.
Carti's streams dont come with names attached.

Now to be fair, Carti's records have shaped more of this era's sound than seventy tapes will, and scarcity is a real strategy that pays enormously when you can hold that much attention. That attention is the harder thing to build.
And seventy releases only works if you already have people who will buy the next one without hearing it first.

But if a man in Atlanta with no label deal can build a buyer base seventy releases deep, the question aint how do I get more plays.
It's how many of your own buyers have bought more than once, and whether anything you use could even tell you.

I built a free Proof of Demand Test Builder that sets up a test to find out if fans will pay for it before you make it.
