# Mach-Hommy: he set the price himself

**SCRIPT:**

Mach-Hommy sold a CD for 300 dollars a copy. Then he put out a record for 999.
Everybody else was giving music away free. So was he overcharging?
The answer should change how independent artists price everything they own.

Let's break it down.

Most artists find out what their music is worth after the fact. You put it out, the market decides, and whatever it decides is what you get.
Mach-Hommy dont do that. He decides first.

In 2016 he pressed 187 copies of H.B.O. and sold them at 300 apiece. Not an auction, not a bidding war. He named the number.
In 2017 he put out The G.A.T. as a vinyl record only, priced at 999 dollars. Most of his catalog never went to streaming at all.

People said he was crazy. Nine hundred and ninety nine dollars for a record when the whole industry had trained everybody to expect it free.

Now here's the thing nobody puts side by side.
That same record came up for auction in November 2025. Go look at what it went for.
He wasn't guessing at that price. But he was wrong about it.

Hold that thought.
You dont need to market to fans. You need a market FOR fans.
Most artists have tools for getting heard and nothing at all for pricing what they own.
That's what the CRWN app is built around: membership tiers and a vault your top members pay to get into.
ANYWAY.

So was Mach-Hommy overcharging at 999?
That record sold at auction for 24,400 dollars.

And here's the crazy part. He wasn't overcharging. He undercharged by about 24 times.
The man everybody called crazy for asking 999 left twenty three thousand dollars on the table, because he priced it lower than his own fans would go.

Go back to them 187 CDs too. 187 people times 300 dollars is 56,100 dollars from one release.
At the penny a month per listener that streaming pays, you'd need a million monthly listeners for about five and a half months to see that.

Now to be fair, streaming is what got most of us to Mach in the first place, and Pray for Haiti being up there is part of why anybody was bidding at all. Discovery did its job.
But if you own something nobody else can sell, the question aint how many people hear it.
It's what the ones who already love it would pay, and whether you asked for enough.
