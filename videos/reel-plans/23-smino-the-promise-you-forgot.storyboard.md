## Story summary

Smino went independent and has the fanbase that makes it work. The moment fans pay monthly,
he owes them things on a schedule, and a forgotten promise makes them quietly stop paying.
The same 100 fans at the same $10 are worth $3,000 if they drift after three months and
$36,000 if they stay three years. The Promise Calendar keeps those obligations out of his head;
a free calculator maps the viewer's own fan business.

## Causal chain

Smino goes independent -> fans begin paying monthly -> monthly payment creates recurring
obligations -> promises can be forgotten -> fans quietly cancel -> retention changes what the
same fans are worth -> the Promise Calendar organizes those obligations -> calculator CTA.

## Concepts considered

Seven idea-level routes. Each is written to the same fields.

### A. The missing-value equation

- **Core idea:** a visual sum built from objects: Smino + a block of 100 fans = a blank "?"
  value.
- **Frame 1:** Smino's portrait on the left, a plus sign, a tidy block of 100 fan figures, an
  equals sign, an empty gold price slot.
- **First second:** the fans flip to show coins; the slot flickers.
- **Through 5s:** digits roll in the slot and never settle.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH above; 100 PAYING FANS under the block; WORTH over the slot.
- **Transitions:** mostly state changes inside a fixed layout.
- **Scroll-stop:** instant readability.
- **Continuation:** the unresolved slot.
- **Sound-off:** fully readable: "Smino + 100 fans = ?".
- **Risk:** it IS a slide. A static diagram in a finance-meme grammar; three small subjects;
  nothing unfolds; no independence without a fourth element. Fails "reject: static title cards,
  explanations before questions".

### B. The price tag over the crowd

- **Core idea:** one enormous blank price tag hangs over Smino's crowd.
- **Frame 1:** Smino on stage, the crowd below, a giant blank tag on a string above them.
- **First second:** the tag spins toward camera, still blank.
- **Through 5s:** the tag's string is traced up to... nothing new; the tag swings.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH on the tag; others pinned below.
- **Transitions:** the tag moves; the world does not change.
- **Scroll-stop:** a price tag is a universal "value" signal.
- **Continuation:** the blank tag.
- **Sound-off:** "what is this crowd worth?"
- **Risk:** it puts a price on the FANS as merchandise, the opposite of the Fan Economy
  thesis ("a market FOR fans"); no progression (one idea, re-shown); no independence. Rejected
  on tone and on "the same hook in several compositions".

### C. The balance scale

- **Core idea:** Smino weighs his fans: 100 tiny fans on one pan, the other pan hidden under a
  cloth.
- **Frame 1:** Smino holding a brass balance; the fan pan low, the covered pan high.
- **First second:** the covered pan starts to sink, cloth still on.
- **Through 5s:** the beam tips back and forth, never settling.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH, 100 PAYING FANS on the fan pan, WORTH on the cloth.
- **Transitions:** a real mechanical cause and effect (weight shifts the beam).
- **Scroll-stop:** a strong, old metaphor for worth.
- **Continuation:** what is under the cloth?
- **Sound-off:** "weighing his fans against something hidden".
- **Risk:** a scale says "compared WITH something". The viewer asks "against what?", a question
  the video never answers, which fails truthfulness and continuity. A tipping scale also
  pre-empts the two-outcomes reveal. No independence.

### D. The strings become fan lines

- **Core idea:** independence physically turns into direct fan economics: marionette strings
  snap, and the cut strings fall and reconnect to fans.
- **Frame 1:** Smino as a marionette, strings from his wrists and shoulders up to a controller
  bar held by an unseen hand.
- **First second:** the strings snap; the bar drops away.
- **Through 5s:** the loose strings fall and re-attach downward, one to each of 100 fans; each
  connection lights gold; a value readout climbs above him.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH, SMINO, 100 PAYING FANS, WORTH, pinned as spoken.
- **Transitions:** excellent: one line literally becomes another kind of line.
- **Scroll-stop:** an artist on puppet strings is arresting.
- **Continuation:** what are those new lines worth?
- **Sound-off:** "freed from control, now tied to his fans".
- **Risk:** FRAME 1 carries the wrong stakes. A marionette reads "who controls Smino?", not
  "what are his fans worth?". The fans and the money arrive only at ~1.5s, so frame 1 fails
  "what is at stake" and wastes the most valuable instant. Its strength (independence as a
  transformation) is worth borrowing.

### E. The coin hands, pull-back

- **Core idea:** one fan's raised coin, then the scale: a hundred of them.
- **Frame 1:** extreme close-up of a fan's hand holding a gold coin up toward Smino, small, on
  stage.
- **First second:** pull back: 100 raised coins.
- **Through 5s:** the coins lift and stream to him and stack.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH, then pinned labels.
- **Transitions:** a genuine scale reveal.
- **Scroll-stop:** a fan paying an artist, up close.
- **Continuation:** how much do they add up to?
- **Sound-off:** "fans paying him, adding up".
- **Risk:** Smino is small and distant in frame 1 (fails subject recognition in the first
  instant); the pull-back is the "zoom" the rules warn about unless the count is the meaning;
  no independence.

### F. The two hidden futures

- **Core idea:** one block of 100 fans at a fork; two paths lead off-frame to ends you cannot
  see.
- **Frame 1:** the 100-fan block at the fork, Smino at its head.
- **First second:** the paths extend.
- **Through 5s:** the fans begin splitting; both ends stay hidden.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH, WORTH.
- **Transitions:** strong: the split is the meaning.
- **Scroll-stop:** intrigue.
- **Continuation:** where does each path end?
- **Sound-off:** "one group, two outcomes".
- **Risk:** it leaks the withheld variable (that there are two outcomes and that staying
  decides them), which the script withholds until ~0:50 ("I ran the two versions") and ~2:10.
  It also steps on the next line's own curiosity ("comes down to one thing"). The truthful
  payoff becomes predictable. Rejected on withholding.

### G. His fans in his own hands (SELECTED)

(Revision 1 below; refined in revisions 2 and 3, locked as revision 3.)

- **Core idea:** a scale contradiction that is also an idiom. Smino, giant and recognizable,
  holds exactly 100 tiny fans standing in the open palm of his own hand. "In his own hands"
  says independence without a word; the fans in his palm say "his fans"; the question says
  "how much". Borrowed from D: the snapped strings, carried as subordinate evidence of the
  control he left.
- **Frame 1:** Smino from the chest up, his open palm pushed toward the viewer, 100 tiny people
  standing on it in a neat block, looking up at him. Cut marionette strings dangle from his
  wrist.
- **First second:** a ripple runs across the tiny crowd as every fan lifts a small gold coin.
  They pay.
- **Through 5s:** the coins rise off the palm into one gold column that climbs beside his face,
  past the broken controller bar where the strings used to lead; a price marker at the column's
  top spins and never lands.
- **Text:** HOW MUCH (0.0), SMINO (1.36), 100 PAYING FANS (2.14), WORTH (3.92, gold), pinned to
  objects as he says them.
- **Transitions:** one world, continuous: palm -> coins -> column -> the unresolved top.
- **Scroll-stop:** the scale contradiction (a giant artist, a hundred tiny people in his hand)
  and a recognizable face in the same instant.
- **Continuation:** how high does that column go?
- **Sound-off:** "Smino holds his own fans; they pay; it adds up; to how much?"
- **Risk:** 100 tiny figures on a palm must still read as PEOPLE at thumbnail size; a
  foreshortened hand is a hard drawing. Mitigations are in the Frame 1 design.

## Concept comparison

- **Instant comprehension:** A and G read in under a second; C, D and E need a beat. F reads
  fast but says too much.
- **Subject recognition:** G (a large face) is best. D is good. A is portrait-in-a-diagram. B,
  E and F make Smino small.
- **Curiosity:** G, D and F compound. A and B hold one question and repeat it.
- **Stakes:** G shows value arriving as the fans pay. B shows value by pricing the fans (the
  wrong tone). C implies a comparison that does not exist.
- **Progression:** D, F and G unfold one world. A and B re-show the same idea.
- **Sound-off performance:** A, F and G survive muted. D needs its frame 1 explained. C invites
  the wrong question.
- **Simplicity:** A and B are simplest but static. G is two subjects (Smino and the fans in his
  hand) plus one growing object.
- **Withholding:** F leaks the mechanism. G shows value growing without a number, a second
  outcome or its cause.
- **Continuity into the script:** G's palm crowd becomes the 100-fan block and its gold column
  becomes the column of the reveal (calibration frame 8), and the snapped strings hand off to
  the next line, "Smino went independent" (8.07s). D shares the strings. F pre-empts 0:50.
- **Truthfulness:** see the gate below. G asks what the script answers. C and F imply things the
  video answers differently.
- **Calibration fit:** all are drawable in the v5 look. G and D are closest to the approved frames.

Rejected: A (a slide), B (prices fans, no progression), C (asks "against what?"), D (frame 1 has
the wrong stakes), E (Smino too small at frame 1), F (leaks the withheld variable). Kept: G, with
D's strings folded in as a subordinate detail rather than a second subject.

## Selected direction

Smino holds his fans in his own hand. They pay him, and their coins pile up right there in the
same palm, beside them. The pile grows one wave of payments at a time. At five seconds the
next wave is still in the air, so the total is growing and not yet known, and nothing
suggests how large it ends up. Everything that matters (the fans, their money, the pile)
stays in HIS hand. Independence is not argued here; the next anchor, "Going Indie", makes it
explicit right after the hook.

## Frame 1

- **Visual state:** the fans in his hand, before anything moves.
- **Composition:** vertical, filled edge to edge. Smino from mid-chest up, slightly low
  camera, big rounded afro on the solid lighter-amber circle (calibration), background plain
  dark burnt orange. His right forearm comes toward the viewer and his open palm fills roughly
  the lower 40% of the frame, foreshortened, larger than his face. On the palm stands ONE
  compact group of fans, shoulder to shoulder in a tight cluster (roughly a dozen to twenty
  figures, never counted). Each figure is big enough that its head, shoulders and raised face
  read as a PERSON at thumbnail size. They wear light, warm clothes (cream, tan, white), so the
  group reads as one pale shape against the mid-tone skin of the palm. A small pool of warm light
  falls on the group. His face looks down at them: calm, assured, a faint half smile (Smino wins).
  Nothing else is in the frame: no strings, no props.
- **Focal point:** the lit group of fans on his palm. His face is the second subject.
- **Text shown:** HOW MUCH (large, white), top of the frame, over the dark orange beside the
  amber circle.
- **Understood instantly:** a recognizable artist holding a group of his fans in his own hand; a
  question about how much.
- **Unanswered:** how much WHAT? (money, answered at 0.5s), and how much in total?
- **Why it stops the scroll:** an impossible, instantly legible image: a giant artist cradling
  real people in his palm. It is not a portrait, an ad, a chart or a title card.
- **Thumbnail check:** at 1/9 of a phone it must read as face + open hand + a pale cluster of
  heads. A compact group of large figures survives that size where a 100-dot grid would not.

## First second

- **Starting state:** Frame 1.
- **Transition:** a wave runs through the group, front to back: every fan lifts a gold coin
  overhead (~0.4s).
- **Resulting state:** the same group, every hand holding up a gold coin toward him.
- **New information:** these are PAYING fans; the money is theirs and it is meant for him.
- **Why curiosity increases:** "how much" now has a unit (money). The viewer wants to know what
  it adds up to.

- **CONTINUITY:** the same hand, the same group, the same camera.

### STATE 2 (0.50-1.30): The paying crowd

- **Shows:** the group with coins raised; his gaze still on them.

### STATE 3 (1.30-2.70): The first pile

- **Shows:** the group and a small coin pile side by side on his palm.

- **FROM:** the first pile, the fans' hands empty.
- **TO:** the pile one layer taller.

  them onto the pile, which gains a layer. The second wave is identical in rhythm to the first.
- **MEANING ADDED:** they pay again; the value accumulates wave by wave (it recurs).
- **RETENTION PURPOSE:** the total is growing. How many more waves?
- **CONTINUITY:** the same group, the same gesture, the same pile.

### STATE 4 (2.70-3.90): The pile grows

- **Shows:** a taller, still modest pile beside the group (it stays well below his face).
- **Visual action:** the pile settles after the second wave; the fans' hands come down.
- **Text shown:** HOW MUCH; 100 PAYING FANS
- **New information:** it is recurring, and it adds up.
- **Reason to stay:** the pile has a rhythm and no ceiling in sight.
- **Withheld:** its eventual size.

### TRANSITION 4 -> 5

- **FROM:** the pile at rest.
- **TO:** a third wave of coins caught in mid-air above the pile.
- **TRANSITION MECHANIC:** the fans raise and release a third wave. As the coins rise off their

  between the fans and the pile, not yet landed.
- **MEANING ADDED:** the total is still being added to; the final amount is not decided yet.
- **RETENTION PURPOSE:** the answer is literally in the air. The viewer waits for it to land.
- **CONTINUITY:** the same gesture and the same coins; the pile stays in frame.

### STATE 5 (3.90-5.66): The coins in the air

- **Shows:** the group with arms up, a wave of coins suspended above the pile, the pile with its

- **Visual action:** the suspended coins drift almost imperceptibly (alive, not frozen); nothing
  lands.
- **Text shown:** HOW MUCH; 100 PAYING FANS; WORTH (gold) appears at 3.92s, pinned beside the
  pile and the coins in the air.
- **New information:** WORTH turns "how much" into value: what these 100 paying fans add up to.
- **Reason to stay:** the addition is unfinished.
- **Withheld:** every figure; how many waves there will be; two outcomes; what decides it.

### TRANSITION 5 -> 6 (5.66-6.16, the pause before "You're about to find out")

- **FROM:** Smino looking down at the coins in his palm.
- **TO:** Smino looking straight at the viewer, the coins still in the air.
- **TRANSITION MECHANIC:** his eyes lift from his palm to the camera, with his head following

  zooms, nothing new enters the frame.
- **MEANING ADDED:** the unresolved question is handed to the viewer: he knows, and you are
  about to.
- **RETENTION PURPOSE:** direct eye contact is a fresh pull at the exact moment the spoken
  question ends. The hook ends on a challenge, not a freeze.
- **CONTINUITY:** everything carries: the same framing, the same palm, the same group, the same
  coins in mid-air.

### STATE 6 (6.16, handoff): Eyes on the viewer

- **Shows:** Smino meeting the viewer's eyes over his open palm; the coins still suspended.
- **Text shown:** HOW MUCH; 100 PAYING FANS; WORTH.
- **Reason to stay:** he is about to answer ("You're about to find out", 6.16-7.42s).
- **Withheld:** the answer.

This is the last state of the opening. What happens on and after "You're about to find out",

### Full-opening gate (0:00-6.16)

- **Who:** Smino.
- **What question:** how much 100 paying fans add up to.
- **Why it matters:** the money is real, his, and still arriving.
- **Answer:** none, and no size cue.
- **Progression:** it never stops. Waves, then the suspended wave, then his eyes to the viewer;

- **Chaos check:** passes. Two subjects, one purposeful repeated gesture.
- **Rejection list:** clear.
- **Pass.**

### Transition gate (all five)

- **Communicates something:** each one does (paying; landing in his hand; recurring; not
  finished; handed to the viewer).
- **Keeps or deepens the gap:** yes, each time.
- **Clarifies cause, scale or progression:** cause (payment -> pile), progression (wave by wave)

- **Continuity:** one camera and one world throughout.
- **Adds a reason to stay:** yes, each time.
- **Would a plain cut be weaker?** Yes, for every one. Cutting between states would lose that the

  break the eye contact that sets up "You're about to find out".
- **Pass.**

### Sound-off gate (voice, music, SFX and captions off)

again, and it piles up. How much are 100 paying fans worth? The next payment is still in the air,

- The primary gap stays open, with no figure and no second outcome.

- **Pass.**
